Most educators who try AI and give up on it make the same mistake: they treat it like a search bar. Type a question, get an answer, hope it's usable.

It rarely is. Not because the AI is bad — because nobody gave it a job.

## The Agent Charter

In Chapter 2 of Sunday Is Yours, I walk through something I call the **Agent Charter** — four elements that turn AI from a guessing game into an actual employee on your team:

1. **Job Description** — what this AI agent's role actually is (lesson drafter, comms triager, CRM updater — pick one job, not five)
2. **Non-Negotiables** — the lines it never crosses (reading levels, standards alignment, your tone, never sending anything without your review)
3. **Tone & Voice** — how it sounds when it writes for you or your students
4. **Output Format** — exactly how the work needs to land so you're not reformatting everything yourself

Here's a stripped-down version of the kind of charter that goes in the workbook, written for a teacher building a lesson-drafting agent:

> **Job:** Draft first-pass lesson plans aligned to my state standards, in my subject area, for 46-minute periods.
> **Non-negotiables:** Never invent content I haven't approved. Never finalize — draft only, I review everything.
> **Tone:** Clear, age-appropriate, no fluff.
> **Output format:** Standard lesson plan template — objective, do-now, mini-lesson, practice, exit ticket.

That's it. Four sentences, and suddenly the AI knows what its job is instead of guessing every time you open a new chat.

## Why This Matters More Than the Prompt Itself

Chapter 3 builds on this with what I call **The Command Language** — a 5-part structure (Intent, Inputs, Constraints, Output Format, Approval Gate) that you use every time you actually ask your agent to do something. But none of that works well without the Charter underneath it. The Charter is the job description. The Command Language is how you talk to someone who already knows their job.

This is also where **The Safety Ladder** comes in (Chapter 4) — three levels of how much you hand over: Draft Only, Organize & Tag, Scheduled Output. You decide how much trust your agent earns, and you can always pull it back. Least privilege, every time. Your classroom decisions stay yours.
