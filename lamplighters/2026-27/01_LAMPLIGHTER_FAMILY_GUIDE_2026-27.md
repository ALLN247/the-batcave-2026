## 2 | WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

The Rochester Omega Lamplighters is being built to become the place where young men in Rochester learn to lead, handle their academics, develop maturity, persevere through difficulty, serve their community, understand how money and opportunity actually move, and gain access to rooms they may not have known existed.

### THE NORTH STAR

> A young man should be able to enter this program at 14 unsure of himself and leave at 18 able to lead a room, carry himself with excellence, understand where economic opportunity lives, and know adults across Rochester who will answer his call.

### THE PIPELINE

**Boys 2 Men (ages 9–13) → Rochester Omega Lamplighters (14–18) → Omega Psi Phi (adult)**

This is Rochester's Black male excellence pipeline, and it is already real. Your son is not joining a stage. He is joining a ladder — and this year he is expected to reach down it as well as up it.

### THE LAMPLIGHTER PROMISE

**We develop LEADERS.** They can run the room.
**We develop SCHOLARS.** They handle their business first.
**We develop MEN.** They have maturity, honesty, and staying power.
**We develop PROFESSIONALS.** They understand careers, business, relationships, and ownership.
**We develop ACCESS.** They belong in rooms that can change their lives.

> We are not only trying to produce good young men.
> We are producing young men capable of creating opportunity — for themselves and for the person coming behind them.

## 3 | THE LAMP STANDARD

LAMP is the national Omega Lamplighters framework and it is the spine of everything we do in Rochester.

| Letter | Pillar | What It Means Here |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | **LEADERSHIP** | My motivation. I lead myself first, then I lead others. I can introduce myself, run an agenda, host a guest, and mentor someone younger. |
| **A** | **ACADEMICS** | My foundation. Grades, attendance, test prep, and applications come before everything the program offers. |
| **M** | **MATURITY** | My inspiration. Emotional discipline, honesty, accountability, and the ability to respond instead of react. |
| **P** | **PERSEVERANCE** | My dedication. I finish. I come back after a bad week, a bad grade, or a bad decision. |

### THE ROCHESTER STANDARD

- Be on time.
- Be coachable.
- Tell the truth — especially when it costs you.
- Handle your academics first.
- Dress and speak like the room matters.
- Respect mentors, guests, hosts, facilities, and each other.
- Serve when the chapter serves.
- Represent the Lamplighters online with the same standard you represent it in person.
- Lift the brother next to you. Never leave one behind.
- Leave every space better than you found it.

## 5 | YOUR DEVELOPMENT JOURNEY

Everybody will not start in the same place. That is expected. Our job is to move each young man forward from wherever he begins.

### THE FOUR LEVELS

**LEVEL 1 — I BELONG HERE**
I show up on time and prepared. I can introduce myself with confidence. I know the Creed, the LAMP pillars, and the standard. I participate instead of watching.

**LEVEL 2 — I CONTRIBUTE**
I complete my assignments and service hours. I check in on my assigned brother every week. I can hold a real conversation with an adult professional. I set and track SMART goals in all four LAMP domains.

**LEVEL 3 — I LEAD**
I run a section of a session. I host guests. I facilitate for younger boys at Boys 2 Men. I recruit. I represent the chapter publicly without supervision.

**LEVEL 4 — I BUILD**
I own an initiative — an event, a business idea, a service project, a recruiting class. I create an opportunity for someone else. I leave something behind that outlasts me.

### THE TWO TIERS THIS YEAR

Year Two means the chapter now has two distinct groups, and both matter.

| | **TIER 1 — THE NEW CLASS** | **TIER 2 — LIGHT LEADERS (Returning)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | New applicants and inductees, ages 14–18 | Founding-class Lamplighters returning for Year Two |
| **Focus** | Belonging, standard, Creed, LAMP basics, first service hours, first professional conversation | Leadership reps, facilitation, mentoring B2M, running program sections, college/career capital |
| **Target level by June** | Level 2 | Level 3–4 |
| **Signature responsibility** | Complete the full 18-O artifact portfolio | Lead at least one monthly experience and mentor at least one younger brother |

**Returning Lamplighters are not repeating the year. They are running it.**

## 8 | SHOW ME THE MONEY: THE ECONOMIC CURRICULUM

**The push:** Black people should participate in the economics surrounding the rooms they are invited into — not just attend them.

Golf is the shared laboratory this year because golf is where relationships, money, and access physically sit down together in Rochester. Your son does not need to become a golfer. He needs to become **golf-literate enough to enter the room, and economically literate enough to understand it.**

### THE MONEY TRAIL QUESTIONS

Every visit, clinic, event, mixer, tour, and partnership ends with the same debrief. Your son should be able to answer these about any business he walks into:

1. Who owns it?
2. Who operates it?
3. Who buys it?
4. Who sells it?
5. Who sponsors it?
6. Who markets it?
7. Who gets paid?
8. How could I enter?
9. How could I eventually own?

### THE STUDENT OBSERVATION LENS

| Lens | The Question He Learns to Ask |
|---|---|
| Customer | Who is the customer and what are they actually paying for? |
| Revenue | Where does money enter? What are the major streams? |
| Cost | What has to be paid before this business makes anything? |
| People | Who works here and what skills or relationships did they need? |
| Brand | How does this place attract and keep customers? |
| Technology | What tools, data, or systems make this work? |
| Relationships | Which partnerships or introductions create value? |
| Ownership | Who owns the asset, the company, the brand, the property? |
| Opportunity | What job, business, or partnership could exist here — for me? |

**This debrief should become automatic.** By June, a Lamplighter should be able to walk into any facility and see the economic system, not just the surface.

## 11 | SERVICE, LEADERSHIP & BOYS 2 MEN

Service is not filler. Service is where leadership stops being a word.

### THE B2M NEAR-PEER MENTOR LANE

Coach G directs **Boys 2 Men** — 39 young men ages 9–13 at 585 Dream Camp, operated by Roc Royal Inc. Those boys are the Lamplighters class of 2028, 2029, and 2030. This year, our Lamplighters go get them.

**Once a month, Lamplighters plan and run an 18 Opportunities event for B2M.** Not as helpers. As facilitators.

Alongside UPrep Golf student-athletes, our Lamplighters will:

- Open the session and set the standard
- Teach a scaled-down version of that month's 18-O theme
- Run a station — putting, introductions, the Money Trail, a build challenge
- Handle logistics, welcome, media, and guest care
- Close with the debrief and reflection

**Why this matters:** a 10-year-old who sees a 16-year-old Lamplighter running the room does not have to imagine the pipeline. He is looking at it. And the Lamplighter running that room just earned a leadership rep no worksheet can give him.

**Running an event for a younger brother counts as one of a Lamplighter's own 18 Opportunities.**

### OTHER SERVICE THIS YEAR

Building on Year One — House of Mercy, the Juneteenth Festival, Mission Fulfilled, Wilson Day of Engagement, SUNY Brockport Saturday of Service, and the Halloween project — we will continue chapter service and add family-optional opportunities throughout the year. **Target: 20 documented service hours per Lamplighter.**

## 12 | LIGHT TEAM (STEP)

Step is a percussive art form rooted in African American tradition where the body becomes the instrument. For the Rochester Omega Lamplighters, the Light Team is where discipline, precision, and brotherhood get physical.

**Why it matters:** develops discipline and focus, builds teamwork, improves fitness and coordination, boosts confidence and expression, and gives the chapter a public face at inductions, showcases, and community events.

**Light Team standards:** attendance at scheduled practices, athletic attire, water, full effort, and the same LAMP standard that governs everything else. Practice schedule and locations are confirmed at the start of each semester and posted in the Parents & Guardians WhatsApp group.

## 14 | SELECTION, INDUCTION & YOUR PLACE

### THE PROCESS

Application → Interview (student and family) → Acceptance → Onboarding & permission documents → Induction ceremony → Active membership

### AN HONEST NOTE ON CLASS SIZE

Our chapter committee made a decision in May 2026 that we are keeping: **we will not accept a new class larger than our confirmed volunteer bench can properly serve.** A smaller class done right beats a bigger class done poorly. Class size for 2026–27 will be announced once volunteer commitments are confirmed.

### IF YOUR SON IS SELECTED

He earns the responsibility of representing this chapter — in sessions, service, events, and public spaces. His place is maintained through continued development and the standard, not by the induction alone.

### IF HE IS NOT SELECTED THIS CYCLE

He is not shut out. He can attend open community events, serve at service projects, join family-open activities, keep developing, and apply in the next cycle. Several of our strongest young men started on the outside of the room.

## 16 | PARENT / GUARDIAN GUIDE

### WHAT YOUR SON IS BEING DEVELOPED TO DO

- Lead himself, then lead others
- Handle academics, goals, and applications
- Manage his emotions and take correction
- Finish what he starts
- Serve his community and mentor younger boys
- Speak to adults and follow up professionally
- Understand careers, businesses, money, and ownership
- Enter unfamiliar spaces confidently and respectfully
- Build relationships that outlast the program

### WHAT FAMILIES CAN EXPECT FROM US

- Clear standards and expectations
- A focus on development, not just activities
- Mentorship, exposure, service, and real professional access
- Monthly communication about schedule and major activities
- Intentional career and economic exposure
- Family engagement events — Mother–Son in October, Father/Mentor–Son in January
- A culture of honesty, discipline, respect, excellence, and belonging

### HOW FAMILIES CAN SUPPORT

- Reinforce attendance, academics, communication, and responsibility
- Get him there on time and prepared
- Attend events when possible — presence matters more than perfection
- Respect program decisions and development timelines
- Encourage him to own his responsibilities instead of you carrying them
- Tell us early when something is hard
- Connect the chapter to positive people, businesses, and opportunities
- Serve when you can

### TRANSPORTATION & CONSENT

Families are responsible for transporting their son to and from sessions and events unless a specific event announcement states otherwise in writing. Transportation and consent language is included in the onboarding and permission documents you sign at the start of the year. If transportation is a barrier for your family, **tell us before it becomes an absence** — we will work to solve it.
